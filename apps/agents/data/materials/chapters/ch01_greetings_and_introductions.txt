========================================
CHAPTER 1: GREETINGS AND INTRODUCTIONS
========================================

When you meet someone for the first time, you greet them. In English there are
many ways to say hello. Common greetings include: Hello, Hi, Good morning, Good
afternoon, and Good evening. Good morning is used before noon. Good afternoon
is used from noon until around six o'clock. Good evening is used in the evening.

When you leave someone, you say goodbye. You can also say: Bye, See you later,
See you tomorrow, or Take care.

To introduce yourself, you say: "My name is ..." or "I am ...". To ask someone's
name you say: "What is your name?" or "What's your name?"

To ask how someone is feeling you say: "How are you?" Common answers are: "I am
fine, thank you.", "I am great!", "Not bad, thank you.", or "I am tired today."

Polite words are very important. Please, thank you, you're welcome, sorry, and
excuse me are all polite expressions. You say "please" when asking for something.
You say "thank you" when someone helps you. You say "you're welcome" when someone
thanks you. You say "sorry" when you make a mistake. You say "excuse me" to get
someone's attention or to pass by someone.

Extra practice expressions:
"Nice to meet you." "Nice to meet you too."
"How are you today?" "I am very well, thank you."
"Where are you from?" "I am from Finland."
"How old are you?" "I am ten years old."

Classroom mini dialogue:
Tom: Hello, my name is Tom. What is your name?
Lina: Hi Tom, I am Lina. Nice to meet you.
Tom: Nice to meet you too. How are you?
Lina: I am good, thank you. And you?
Tom: I am fine.

Remember:
Use formal greetings with adults you do not know well.
Use informal greetings with friends and classmates.

More useful introductions:
"This is my friend Emma."
"Let me introduce myself."
"I am in grade five."
"I live in Espoo."
"My favourite subject is English."

Questions people ask when meeting:
"Where do you live?"
"What class are you in?"
"Do you have any hobbies?"
"What is your favourite colour?"
"Can you speak any other languages?"

Possible answers:
"I live near the city centre."
"I am in class 5B."
"My hobby is football."
"My favourite colour is blue."
"I can speak Finnish and a little English."

Telephone greetings:
"Hello, this is Sara speaking."
"May I speak to Leo, please?"
"Please wait a moment."
"He is not here right now."

Polite classroom expressions:
"Could you help me, please?"
"May I ask a question?"
"Thank you for your help."
"Excuse me, I do not understand this task."

Situational examples:
At school: "Good morning, teacher."
At a friend's house: "Hi, thanks for inviting me."
On the phone: "Hello, who is speaking?"
When leaving: "Goodbye, see you tomorrow."

Practice sentences:
"My name is Oliver and I am eleven years old."
"Hello, I am new in this school."
"Nice to meet you. I hope we can be friends."
"Sorry, I did not hear your name."

Mini role-play ideas:
1. Two classmates meet for the first time.
2. A student introduces a friend to a teacher.
3. A child answers the phone politely.

Key idea:
Good greetings are friendly, clear, and polite.
