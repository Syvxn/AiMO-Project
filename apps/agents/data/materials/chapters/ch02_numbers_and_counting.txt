========================================
CHAPTER 2: NUMBERS AND COUNTING
========================================

Numbers in English from one to twenty: one, two, three, four, five, six, seven,
eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen,
eighteen, nineteen, twenty.

Numbers from twenty to one hundred: twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy,
eighty, ninety, one hundred. To say numbers between tens you combine them:
twenty-one, thirty-five, forty-seven, fifty-eight, sixty-three, seventy-nine,
eighty-two, ninety-four.

Ordinal numbers tell the order of things. First (1st), second (2nd), third (3rd),
fourth (4th), fifth (5th), sixth (6th), seventh (7th), eighth (8th), ninth (9th),
tenth (10th). After tenth the pattern becomes: eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth,
fourteenth, fifteenth, twentieth, twenty-first.

We use ordinal numbers to talk about dates and positions. For example: "Today is
the fifth of April." or "She finished in third place."

Zero is the number before one. It is also called nought or "oh" in phone numbers.
A dozen means twelve. A hundred means one hundred. A thousand means one thousand.

Numbers in daily life:
We use numbers for age, time, dates, money, pages, and scores.
Examples: "I am eleven years old." "The lesson starts at nine o'clock."
"My birthday is on the twenty-third of May." "The book has one hundred pages."

Counting patterns:
2, 4, 6, 8 are even numbers.
1, 3, 5, 7 are odd numbers.
Count by fives: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30.
Count by tens: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50.

Useful question forms:
"How many pencils are there?" "There are twelve pencils."
"What number comes after nineteen?" "Twenty."
"What is your phone number?" "It is zero four zero..."

Larger numbers:
one thousand, two thousand, ten thousand, one million.
Examples: 1,250 = one thousand two hundred and fifty.
2,018 = two thousand and eighteen.

Using numbers with money:
One euro, two euros, fifty cents.
"The apple costs one euro."
"The book costs twelve euros."

Using numbers with time:
7:00 = seven o'clock
7:15 = quarter past seven
7:30 = half past seven
7:45 = quarter to eight

Using numbers in maths:
plus, minus, times, divided by, equals.
"Two plus three equals five."
"Ten minus four equals six."
"Five times two equals ten."

Counting objects:
one ball, two balls, three books, four chairs, five apples.
Remember to use plural nouns when the number is more than one.

Number facts:
There are 60 minutes in an hour.
There are 24 hours in a day.
There are 7 days in a week.
There are 12 months in a year.

Practice questions:
"How many legs does a spider have?"
"What is the tenth month of the year?"
"What number is between forty-nine and fifty-one?"

Challenge counting:
Count backwards from twenty to zero.
Count in twos from two to twenty.
Count in tens from ten to one hundred.
