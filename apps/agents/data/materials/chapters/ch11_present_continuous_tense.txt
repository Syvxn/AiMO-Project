========================================
CHAPTER 11: PRESENT CONTINUOUS TENSE
========================================

The present continuous tense (also called present progressive) is used to describe
actions that are happening right now, at this moment. It is formed with the verb
to be (am, is, are) plus the -ing form of the main verb.

Positive form: I am eating lunch. She is watching TV. He is playing football.
We are studying English. They are sleeping.

Negative form: I am not eating. She isn't watching TV. He isn't playing.
We aren't studying. They aren't sleeping.

Question form: Am I late? Is she watching TV? Is he playing outside? Are we
finished? Are they coming?

Short answers: "Yes, I am." / "No, I am not." "Yes, she is." / "No, she isn't."

Spelling rules for the -ing form:
- Most verbs: add -ing → eat → eating, sleep → sleeping, read → reading.
- Verbs ending in silent -e: drop the e and add -ing → write → writing,
  make → making, come → coming.
- Short verbs with one vowel between two consonants: double the last consonant
  and add -ing → run → running, swim → swimming, sit → sitting.

Present continuous is also used to talk about plans in the near future:
"We are visiting our grandparents next weekend.",
"She is starting a new school in September."

The difference between present simple and present continuous:
Present simple — habits and routines: "I walk to school every day."
Present continuous — happening now: "I am walking to school right now."

Signal words for present continuous:
now, right now, at the moment, today, this week.

More sentence examples:
"They are doing their homework at the moment."
"I am not wearing a jacket today."
"Is your teacher writing on the board now?"

Common mistakes to avoid:
Incorrect: "She watching TV."
Correct: "She is watching TV."
Incorrect: "I am play football."
Correct: "I am playing football."

Short practice prompts:
Describe three things happening in your classroom now.
Write one positive, one negative, and one question sentence.

More examples with am, is, and are:
"I am reading a story."
"She is drawing a picture."
"They are playing in the yard."
"We are learning new words today."

Negative examples:
"I am not sleeping."
"He is not riding a bike."
"They are not watching a film now."

Question examples:
"Are you listening to the teacher?"
"Is the baby sleeping?"
"Are the children doing their homework?"

When we use this tense:
for actions happening now,
for temporary situations,
and for near-future plans.

Temporary situation examples:
"I am staying with my grandparents this week."
"My sister is using my room today."

Spelling review:
dance -> dancing
lie -> lying
tie -> tying
begin -> beginning

Practice prompts:
Look around you and name five actions people are doing.
Change a present simple sentence into present continuous.
