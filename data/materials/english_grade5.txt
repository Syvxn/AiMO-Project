ENGLISH FOR FINNISH 5TH GRADERS
================================
Study material for quiz generation and RAG retrieval.
Each section is a chapter covering a core English topic.


========================================
CHAPTER 1: GREETINGS AND INTRODUCTIONS
========================================

When you meet someone for the first time, you greet them. In English there are
many ways to say hello. Common greetings include: Hello, Hi, Good morning, Good
afternoon, and Good evening. Good morning is used before noon. Good afternoon
is used from noon until around six o'clock. Good evening is used in the evening.

When you leave someone, you say goodbye. You can also say: Bye, See you later,
See you tomorrow, or Take care.

To introduce yourself, you say: "My name is ..." or "I am ...". To ask someone's
name you say: "What is your name?" or "What's your name?"

To ask how someone is feeling you say: "How are you?" Common answers are: "I am
fine, thank you.", "I am great!", "Not bad, thank you.", or "I am tired today."

Polite words are very important. Please, thank you, you're welcome, sorry, and
excuse me are all polite expressions. You say "please" when asking for something.
You say "thank you" when someone helps you. You say "you're welcome" when someone
thanks you. You say "sorry" when you make a mistake. You say "excuse me" to get
someone's attention or to pass by someone.


========================================
CHAPTER 2: NUMBERS AND COUNTING
========================================

Numbers in English from one to twenty: one, two, three, four, five, six, seven,
eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen,
eighteen, nineteen, twenty.

Numbers from twenty to one hundred: twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy,
eighty, ninety, one hundred. To say numbers between tens you combine them:
twenty-one, thirty-five, forty-seven, fifty-eight, sixty-three, seventy-nine,
eighty-two, ninety-four.

Ordinal numbers tell the order of things. First (1st), second (2nd), third (3rd),
fourth (4th), fifth (5th), sixth (6th), seventh (7th), eighth (8th), ninth (9th),
tenth (10th). After tenth the pattern becomes: eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth,
fourteenth, fifteenth, twentieth, twenty-first.

We use ordinal numbers to talk about dates and positions. For example: "Today is
the fifth of April." or "She finished in third place."

Zero is the number before one. It is also called nought or "oh" in phone numbers.
A dozen means twelve. A hundred means one hundred. A thousand means one thousand.


========================================
CHAPTER 3: COLOURS
========================================

Colours are an important part of English vocabulary. The basic colours are: red,
blue, yellow, green, orange, purple, pink, brown, black, white, and grey.

Red is the colour of fire engines, roses, and blood. Blue is the colour of the sky
and the sea. Yellow is the colour of the sun and bananas. Green is the colour of
grass and trees. Orange is both a colour and a fruit. Purple is a mix of red and
blue. Pink is a light red colour. Brown is the colour of chocolate and mud.
Black is the darkest colour. White is the lightest colour. Grey is between black
and white.

Some colours have light and dark versions. Light blue is called sky blue or baby
blue. Dark blue is called navy blue. Light green can be called lime green.
Dark green is called forest green.

To describe something using a colour you put the colour before the noun: a red
apple, a blue sky, a yellow banana, a green leaf, a black cat, a white dog.
You can also say: "The apple is red." or "The sky is blue."


========================================
CHAPTER 4: DAYS OF THE WEEK AND MONTHS
========================================

There are seven days in a week. The days of the week in English are: Monday,
Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday. Monday is the first
day of the school week. Saturday and Sunday are the weekend.

Each day has a short form: Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun.

There are twelve months in a year. The months are: January, February, March,
April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, and December.
January is the first month. December is the last month.

Seasons of the year: spring (March–May), summer (June–August), autumn or fall
(September–November), and winter (December–February). In Finland, winter is cold
and snowy. Summer is warm and sunny.

To say what day it is: "Today is Monday." To ask what day it is: "What day is it
today?" To say what month it is: "It is April." To ask what the date is:
"What is the date today?"

Common prepositions with time: on Monday, on Tuesday, in January, in summer,
at noon, at midnight, at three o'clock, in the morning, in the afternoon,
in the evening, at night.


========================================
CHAPTER 5: SCHOOL AND CLASSROOM VOCABULARY
========================================

The classroom is where students learn. In a classroom you can find: a board
(blackboard or whiteboard), a teacher's desk, student desks, chairs, a projector,
windows, and doors.

School subjects taught in Finnish 5th grade include: English, Finnish, mathematics
(maths), science, history, arts and crafts, physical education (PE), music, and
religion or ethics.

Classroom objects: a pencil, a pen, a rubber (eraser), a ruler, a pencil case,
a notebook, a textbook, a schoolbag (backpack), scissors, glue, and coloured
pencils.

Useful classroom phrases: "May I go to the toilet?", "Can you repeat that,
please?", "I don't understand.", "Can you help me?", "What does this word mean?",
"How do you spell that?", "Is this correct?", "Can I borrow a pencil?"

The teacher gives homework and tests. Students study, read, write, listen, and
speak in lessons. A lesson is usually forty-five minutes long. There are breaks
between lessons. Lunch break is the longest break in the school day.


========================================
CHAPTER 6: FAMILY MEMBERS
========================================

A family is a group of people who are related to each other. Family members in
English: mother (mum/mom), father (dad), sister, brother, grandmother (grandma),
grandfather (grandpa), aunt, uncle, cousin, daughter, son, wife, husband, and baby.

A nuclear family usually means parents and their children. An extended family
includes grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins.

To describe family relationships you say: "She is my sister.", "He is my father.",
"They are my parents.", "She is my grandmother on my mother's side."

Possessive adjectives used with family: my mother, your father, his sister,
her brother, our grandparents, their children. The possessive 's is also common:
"This is Tom's sister." or "Anna's mum works in a hospital."

Common questions about family: "How many brothers and sisters do you have?",
"Do you have any siblings?", "What does your father do?", "Where does your
family live?"


========================================
CHAPTER 7: FOOD AND DRINKS
========================================

Food vocabulary is very useful in everyday life. Common foods: bread, butter,
cheese, milk, eggs, rice, pasta, potatoes, chicken, fish, beef, pork, soup,
salad, pizza, sandwich, and cereal.

Fruits: apple, banana, orange, strawberry, blueberry, grapes, pear, lemon,
mango, and pineapple. Vegetables: carrot, onion, potato, tomato, cucumber,
broccoli, peas, corn, garlic, and cabbage.

Drinks: water, juice, milk, tea, hot chocolate, lemonade, and smoothie.
Children usually drink water, milk, or juice with meals.

At a restaurant or café you can say: "Can I have a glass of water, please?",
"I would like a cheese sandwich.", "What do you recommend?", "The bill, please."

Meal times: breakfast is in the morning, lunch is at midday, dinner (or supper)
is in the evening. A snack is a small amount of food eaten between meals.

To say what you like: "I love pizza.", "I like apples.", "I don't like onions.",
"I hate broccoli." To say what you are eating: "I am eating a banana.",
"She is drinking milk."


========================================
CHAPTER 8: ANIMALS
========================================

Animals are living creatures. They are divided into several groups. Mammals are
warm-blooded animals that feed their young with milk. Examples: dog, cat, horse,
cow, elephant, lion, dolphin, and whale. Birds have feathers and wings. Examples:
eagle, penguin, sparrow, robin, duck, owl, and parrot.

Reptiles have scales and are cold-blooded. Examples: snake, lizard, crocodile,
and turtle. Amphibians live on land and in water. Examples: frog and salamander.
Fish live in water and breathe through gills. Examples: salmon, tuna, goldfish,
and shark. Insects have six legs. Examples: butterfly, ant, bee, and mosquito.

Common pets: dog, cat, rabbit, hamster, guinea pig, goldfish, budgerigar (budgie),
and turtle. Dogs are often called man's best friend. Cats are independent and clean.

Wild animals found in Finland: elk (moose), reindeer, brown bear, wolf, fox,
lynx, beaver, otter, and many species of birds.

Animal sounds in English: a dog barks, a cat meows, a cow moos, a sheep bleats,
a horse neighs, a duck quacks, a lion roars, a bird sings/chirps.

To describe animals you can use adjectives: fast, slow, big, small, dangerous,
friendly, fluffy, scaly, and wild.


========================================
CHAPTER 9: WEATHER AND SEASONS
========================================

The weather tells us what the sky and air are like outside. Common weather words:
sunny, cloudy, windy, rainy, snowy, foggy, stormy, and icy. To ask about the
weather: "What is the weather like today?" or "How is the weather?"

Weather descriptions: "It is sunny today.", "It is raining.", "It is snowing.",
"It is cold and windy.", "It is warm and cloudy.", "There is a storm coming."

Temperature is measured in degrees Celsius (°C) in Finland and most of Europe,
and in degrees Fahrenheit (°F) in the United States. Water freezes at 0°C and
boils at 100°C. A warm summer day in Finland might be 25°C. A cold winter day
might be -15°C.

Season vocabulary:
Spring — warm, flowers bloom, birds return, rainy, green.
Summer — hot, sunny, long days, holidays, swimming, light nights.
Autumn — cool, leaves change colour (red, orange, yellow), windy, shorter days.
Winter — cold, snow, ice, dark, short days, skiing, Christmas.

Useful weather vocabulary: umbrella (used when it rains), raincoat, boots, sunglasses
(used when it is sunny), scarf, gloves, and hat (used when it is cold).


========================================
CHAPTER 10: PRESENT SIMPLE TENSE
========================================

The present simple tense is used to talk about habits, routines, and facts that
are always true. It is one of the most important tenses in English.

Positive sentences: I eat breakfast every morning. She reads books. He plays
football on Saturdays. They go to school by bus. We live in Helsinki.

Negative sentences: use do not (don't) for I, you, we, they. Use does not
(doesn't) for he, she, it. I don't eat meat. She doesn't like coffee. They
don't watch TV on weekdays. He doesn't play video games.

Question form: use Do for I, you, we, they. Use Does for he, she, it.
"Do you like pizza?" "Does she speak French?" "Do they have a dog?"
"Does he play music?"

Short answers: "Yes, I do." / "No, I don't." "Yes, she does." / "No, she doesn't."

Third person singular (he, she, it) adds -s or -es to the verb:
play → plays, eat → eats, run → runs, watch → watches, go → goes, do → does.

Common time expressions used with present simple: every day, every week, always,
usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, on Mondays, in the morning.

Example sentences with time expressions: "I always brush my teeth before bed.",
"She usually walks to school.", "He sometimes forgets his homework.",
"They never eat vegetables.", "We often play outside after school."


========================================
CHAPTER 11: PRESENT CONTINUOUS TENSE
========================================

The present continuous tense (also called present progressive) is used to describe
actions that are happening right now, at this moment. It is formed with the verb
to be (am, is, are) plus the -ing form of the main verb.

Positive form: I am eating lunch. She is watching TV. He is playing football.
We are studying English. They are sleeping.

Negative form: I am not eating. She isn't watching TV. He isn't playing.
We aren't studying. They aren't sleeping.

Question form: Am I late? Is she watching TV? Is he playing outside? Are we
finished? Are they coming?

Short answers: "Yes, I am." / "No, I am not." "Yes, she is." / "No, she isn't."

Spelling rules for the -ing form:
- Most verbs: add -ing → eat → eating, sleep → sleeping, read → reading.
- Verbs ending in silent -e: drop the e and add -ing → write → writing,
  make → making, come → coming.
- Short verbs with one vowel between two consonants: double the last consonant
  and add -ing → run → running, swim → swimming, sit → sitting.

Present continuous is also used to talk about plans in the near future:
"We are visiting our grandparents next weekend.",
"She is starting a new school in September."

The difference between present simple and present continuous:
Present simple — habits and routines: "I walk to school every day."
Present continuous — happening now: "I am walking to school right now."


========================================
CHAPTER 12: COMMON ADJECTIVES AND OPPOSITES
========================================

Adjectives are words that describe nouns. They tell us more about people, places,
animals, and things. Adjectives usually come before the noun or after the verb
to be.

Examples before the noun: a tall boy, a small cat, a happy girl, an old house,
a beautiful flower, a scary monster, a fast car.
Examples after to be: "The boy is tall.", "The cat is small.", "She is happy."

Common adjectives and their opposites (antonyms):
big / small — The elephant is big. The mouse is small.
tall / short — My dad is tall. My little sister is short.
fast / slow — Cheetahs are fast. Tortoises are slow.
hot / cold — The soup is hot. The ice cream is cold.
new / old — I have a new phone. My bike is old.
clean / dirty — The classroom is clean. The boots are dirty.
easy / difficult (hard) — This exercise is easy. That maths problem is difficult.
happy / sad — She is happy today. He was sad after the match.
loud / quiet — The concert was loud. The library is quiet.
rich / poor — The king was rich. The beggar was poor.
strong / weak — Bears are strong. Baby kittens are weak.
light / dark — The room is light. The cave is dark.
beautiful / ugly — The sunset is beautiful. The rubbish bin is ugly.
full / empty — The glass is full. The box is empty.
safe / dangerous — The playground is safe. The road is dangerous.

Comparative adjectives are used to compare two things. Add -er to short adjectives:
tall → taller, fast → faster, old → older, cold → colder.
Use more before long adjectives: more beautiful, more dangerous, more interesting.
Example: "She is taller than her brother.", "This book is more interesting than
that one."
